# Non-Anime / Historical Aesthetic Bible Creation

**Added:** 2026-05-19

## Overview
The `sgflix-create-character-bible` skill works for live-action and historical aesthetics (1960s NASA educational films, 1970s grindhouse, 1980s OVA, etc.) with minor adaptations.

## Key Adaptations

- Replace anime-specific language with period film stock references ("16mm Kodak Ektachrome", "visible silver halide grain", "documentary texture").
- For historical subjects, emphasize era-specific tone ("slightly naive optimism", "government-issue", "educational film style", "exploitation aesthetic").
- Environment and prop bibles follow the same 8-page structure when consistency across scenes matters.
- Always perform explicit cross-page consistency checks (helmet style, proportions, color treatment, texture).

## Pitfall
Do not assume pages are consistent just because the subject is the same. Verify key identity elements across all pages before moving to video generation.

## Example Use Case
1960s NASA Space Race Educational Film series — full character + environment + equipment bibles created using this method.

## 1970s Blaxploitation Character Bibles (Added 2026-05-20)

When creating bibles for 1970s Blaxploitation projects:

- Use warm tungsten lighting, deep oranges/browns, high contrast, and visible 16mm/35mm film grain in all prompts.
- Emphasize period-accurate wardrobe (leather trench coats, turtlenecks, high-waisted slacks, gold accessories, afros, bell-bottoms).
- Include "gritty urban decay" and "soul/funk cinematography" language to maintain the exploitation aesthetic.
- For character consistency across shots, always generate the full 8-page bible first before any video work.
- When building internal conflict, tie visual choices to emotional undercurrents (e.g., "calm confident expression with buried sadness").

**Example Character**: Malcolm “Mack” St. Clair (1974 Harlem private investigator) — full 8-page bible successfully created following this structure.
