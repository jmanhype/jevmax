## Premise (locked, rank-gated ADVANCE 7.85)

1959. Starshina Valentina "Valya" Orlova, night-shift switchboard supervisor at
Object 10-D — a secret Soviet lunar listening post — intercepts an American
astronaut's mayday that officially never happened. Both empires want the tape.

## Logline

A Soviet switchboard operator hears a dead American astronaut begging for help
on a frequency that carries no traffic — and keeping the line open will cost
her everything she's ever been ordered to believe.

## Controlling idea

Truth doesn't need permission. When every institution agrees on a lie, one
person holding a line open — a wire, a log, a memory — is the only future the
truth has.

## The lie of the world (series engine)

Both empires, racing for the Moon, have already lost people they never admitted
existed. Frequency 14 megacycles is where the unofficial dead still broadcast.
The mayday Valya hears is one of dozens. **The engine regenerates**: every plug
inserted into the board can surface a new voice, a new coverup, a new order to
forget — and every order to forget costs someone their father, their crew,
their name.

**Section 7** (established EP001, canonized 2026-09-23 after gate REVIEW):
Soviet signals doctrine — an unlogged intercept is treason, by statute.
Brass placard above every cut-key in Signals installations. It is why logging
the mayday is Valya's first act of defiance, and why an erased log entry is a
weapon pointed at whoever signed it.

## Cast

  (`bibles/valentina-orlova/identity_lock.yaml`), Kling Element 322149783311120.
  Father: Pyotr Orlov, radio operator, lost 1953 in the Arctic — officially
  "weather accident." The eyebrow scar (script-only canon): glass from the '53

- **Major Arkady Korabelnikov** (52, signals counterintelligence) — WW2 veteran
  who has buried more truth than Valya has ever heard. Not a cartoon: a man
  caught between the order of the day and an oath he swore in 1945 to men who
  died on the wire. Wants the frequency closed; needs Valya to close it
  quietly. Will become her reluctant shield.
- **Riva Sokolova** (24, junior operator, Valya's rival) — night-shift rival
  who's been secretly ANSWERING the maydays for months: a one-woman
  switchboard of the lost. Warmth under the rivalry; her exposure is mid-season
  rupture.
- **THE VOICE — "SEVEN-THREE"** (American astronaut, Cdr. Johnny Harrow) —
  heard, never seen until finale. The mystery thread: his radio discipline is
  Soviet-trained. Someone on the other side taught the enemy's dead man to
  call home.

## Format decision — both hooks developed

**Vertical short-drama series (9:16, 12 × ~110s) — RECOMMENDED.**
Why: (a) the rank gate scored this concept's ENGINE highest asset (1.81/5) —
the premise earns its keep regenerating, which is a series virtue, not a film
virtue; (b) the stack (short-drama skills, Element anchoring, cliffhanger
bands, 60-credit shots) is built for exactly this cadence; (c) the Cannon Buy
criterion is literally "holds a scrolling viewer" — the vertical audience is
the test's native population. Arc: 12 episodes, hook-first, every button a
threat or reversal.

**Short film (16:9, ~12 min) — viable fallback.**
Compresses eps 1–5, ends on the father-tape reveal as the climax button.
Loses: Riva arc, the tracing pressure, the season engine. Gains: single-view
festival shape; shot 01 already 16:9. Decision reversible at storyboard stage;
bible serves both.
