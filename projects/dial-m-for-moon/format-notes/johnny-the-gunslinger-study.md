# Format note: Johnny the Gunslinger EP11 study (VIKTHOR)

Studied 2026-09-23. Source: "THE BOUNTY" — 83s vertical episode, opens on
cliff-side town at night. Verified frame-by-frame (6 samples across the
runtime); character reads below are confirmed, not assumed.

## What the episode does

- **Silhouette-first character design.** Johnny: tan duster with distinctive
  cape-like shoulder flaps, brown hat, long dark wavy hair. The bounty
  hunter: cream long coat, pale hat, dark ponytail. Both read cleanly from
  wide night establishing shots down to tight rear views. The silhouette
  does the identity work; the model cannot drift far because the shapes
  are too specific to blur.
- **Segmented locations with hard cuts.** Night town approach → saloon
  entrance → bar (whiskey close-up) → interior tension → bedroom with
  broken window → alley standoff → wagon → wagon rolling into the
  distance. No single environment is held long enough for model drift to
  become obvious. The cut is used as a continuity reset.
- **Captioned dialogue, no lip-sync.** The plot turns on a few short,
  decisive captioned lines ("what'll it be" / "whiskey" / "you have two
  choices... join him in the wagon or six feet under"). This is his public
  advice practiced: no "talking statues."
- **Serial branding + open hook.** "THE GUN SLINGER JOHNNY" bullet-hole
  title card, episode title "THE BOUNTY," and a "to be continued" ending
  on the wagon leaving town. The story is still moving when the episode
  ends — the handoff between episodes is narrative, not technical.

## The dual-style correction

VIKTHOR works in **both** styles. The OpenArt AI Ad Awards entry (posted
2026-09-23, ~99s, made with Wan 3.0) is fully photoreal — Times Square,
smartwatch close-ups, domestic kitchen/restaurant, subway — with
consistent characters across locations. The stylized 2D look is his
*serial* choice, not his only capability. Read: the stylized look is a
consistency cheat code (flat colors + strong silhouettes + short beats do
heavy lifting), but he applies the same continuity and camera-control
principles under photorealism's harder constraints. Photoreal serial work
is doable; it just costs more reference discipline.

## What transfers to Dial M for Moon (highest ROI first)

1. **Silhouette-first character design.** Valya's bun / headset / red
   pencil already works this way. Lean harder into one or two oversized,
   unmistakable silhouette elements in prompts and bibles — bigger shapes
   make reference locking more forgiving.
2. **One location (or one clear action) per short segment.** Already our
   practice at shot level (EP003: one room, one action). The episode-level
   version scales it up: use the cut as a continuity reset, never hold one
   generated environment past its beat.
3. **Captioned dialogue discipline.** Matches his public advice; removes
   the talking-statue risk entirely. Dialogue advances plot in short
   decisive lines, not exposition.
4. **End on a forward lean, not a full resolve.** Biggest format shift
   available to us. Our episodes currently close the beat; his leave the
   story still moving. One unresolved thread per episode turns a series of
   shorts into something people feel compelled to return for.
5. **Title-card branding per episode.** Low effort, high serial identity
   value. Episode title + series mark, consistent placement.

## Standing rule adopted

Every episode ends with at least one open thread — a question, a task
unfinished, a door unopened. Resolve the beat, not the story.
